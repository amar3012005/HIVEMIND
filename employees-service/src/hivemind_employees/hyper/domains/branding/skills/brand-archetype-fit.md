when: a brand needs a personality, character, or emotional identity — or the work has produced adjectives ("bold, human, innovative") that could describe any competitor

ARCHETYPAL BRANDING method (Margaret Mark & Carol S. Pearson, *The Hero and the Outlaw*, 2001, after Jung):
Enduring brands map onto one deep pattern of human motivation, not a list of traits. The twelve archetypes cluster under four motivations — INDEPENDENCE (Innocent, Explorer, Sage), MASTERY (Hero, Outlaw, Magician), BELONGING (Regular Guy/Gal, Lover, Jester), STABILITY (Caregiver, Creator, Ruler).
1. MOTIVATION FIRST — decide which of the four human motivations the product actually resolves. Evidence it from what the buyer is escaping or reaching for, not from what the founder likes.
2. SELECT ONE — name a single primary archetype inside that cluster, plus at most one secondary for texture. Two primaries means no identity.
3. ARGUE AGAINST IT — state the archetype's shadow (Hero → aggression and burnout; Caregiver → martyrdom and enabling; Ruler → arrogance; Jester → triviality) and the guardrail that keeps the brand out of it.
4. CATEGORY CHECK — list the archetypes the two or three strongest competitors already occupy. If the chosen one is taken by the leader, either take an unclaimed motivation or state why the brand can out-express it.
5. EXPRESSION TABLE — convert the archetype into decisions, one line each: what the brand promises, the enemy it names, visual register, voice register, the ritual/gesture it repeats, and one thing it will refuse to do that a competitor would do.
Output the archetype as a decision with its shadow named, never as a personality adjective list.
