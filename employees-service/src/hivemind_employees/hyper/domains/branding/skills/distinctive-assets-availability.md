---
when: a brand task is about growth, recognition, logo/identity refresh, or "we need to stand out" — and the plan is drifting toward persuasion instead of being noticed and easy to buy
---

DISTINCTIVE ASSETS + AVAILABILITY method (Byron Sharp, *How Brands Grow*; Romaniuk & Sharp, *How Brands Grow Part 2*; Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets*, Ehrenberg-Bass Institute):
Brands grow by reaching more category buyers, not by deepening loyalty in heavy ones. Two assets do the work: mental availability (the brand comes to mind in a buying situation) and physical availability (it can be bought, in the variant and channel the buyer wants). Differentiation persuades less than distinctiveness makes recognizable.
1. CATEGORY ENTRY POINTS — list the concrete situations that trigger a purchase in this category (cue, occasion, need, place, mood, co-purchase). Name which CEPs the brand currently owns and which are unclaimed. Do not write demographics.
2. ASSET AUDIT — inventory every non-name identifier: colour, shape, typeface, character, sound, layout, tagline. Score each on FAME (share of category buyers who see/hear it and think of this brand) and UNIQUENESS (share who name only this brand). High fame + high uniqueness = protect and repeat unchanged; high fame + low uniqueness = a category cue, not yours; low fame = invest or retire.
3. REACH DECISION — choose the broadest-reach vehicle available, not the most targeted, and state what will be repeated identically across all of them.
4. PHYSICAL AVAILABILITY GAPS — name where a ready buyer currently cannot buy: missing channel, missing size/tier, friction step, geography.
5. CONSISTENCY RULE — write what may never change again, and the one asset the next 12 months will build.
Refuse any recommendation that redesigns a high-fame asset without a growth reason.
