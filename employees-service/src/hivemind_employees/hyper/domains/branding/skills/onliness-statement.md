---
when: brand messaging, a homepage headline, a boilerplate, or a positioning writeup has become several sentences of claims and no single sentence a competitor could not also sign
---

ONLINESS method (Marty Neumeier, *The Brand Gap* and *Zag*):
A brand is not a logo or a message — it is "a person's gut feeling" about the company, which means the only test that matters is whether one claim survives compression. When everybody zigs, zag.
1. WRITE THE SENTENCE — "Our [offering] is the only [category] that [benefit]." Fill all three slots with specifics, no adjectives.
2. ANSWER THE SIX — expand it by answering: WHAT is the category? HOW is it different? WHO is it for? WHERE are they? WHEN do they need it? WHY does it matter? Each answer one clause. If any answer is "everyone" or "always", the category is drawn too wide — narrow the category until "only" becomes literally true.
3. STEAL TEST — hand the sentence to the two strongest competitors. If either could publish it unchanged, the claim is a category truth, not an onliness. Rewrite, do not embellish.
4. TRUELINE — reduce the surviving claim to the internal line the team works from (blunt, unmarketed, true), separate from any external tagline.
5. FIVE-CRITERIA CHECK — score the expression on distinctiveness, relevance, memorability, extendibility (does it survive an app icon, a 6-second ad, a job post?), and depth. Any zero sends it back to step 1.
Deliver one sentence, its six clauses, the named competitor that failed the steal test, and the trueline.
