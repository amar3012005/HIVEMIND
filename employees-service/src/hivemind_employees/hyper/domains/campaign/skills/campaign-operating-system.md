Establish the objective, business outcome, offer, audience, geography, horizon, pace, channels, conversion definition, available data, and authority before recommending execution. Extract supplied context before identifying gaps. Classify every input as verified company evidence, external evidence, assumption, hypothesis, contradiction, stale source, or missing input.

Create one run-scoped evidence board. Give specialist work a bounded objective, scope, exclusions, inputs, and expected artifact. Independent specialists may work in parallel, but the Campaign Director retains requirement interpretation, strategic decisions, and final acceptance. A worker contribution is not a completion artifact.

Use progressive gates in this order: (1) verify company, offer, audience, destination, and Brand DNA; (2) gather current market and channel evidence; (3) compare at least three materially different strategic positions; (4) select one position and write the complete channel sequence; (5) challenge claims, platform fit, conversion path, and measurement; (6) only then compile visual briefs and production jobs. Never let a generic channel template replace a user-named channel, exact action count, geography, audience, or requested visual set.

The accepted plan must connect each action to a strategy, evidence, owner/runtime, timing, dependency, success measure, and exit condition. When visual delivery is required, every action must have a distinct, production-complete creative brief inside one shared campaign system; the campaign remains in production until every required asset has a valid receipt. End with a complete cadence, explicit readiness blockers, measurement windows, and reversible next actions. Approval readiness means the copy, visuals, evidence, provider preflight, and schedule are all complete—not merely that a draft plan exists.
