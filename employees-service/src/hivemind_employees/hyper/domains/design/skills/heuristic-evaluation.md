---
when: an existing screen, flow, or product needs to be evaluated or reviewed for usability and there is no time, budget, or access for user testing
---

HEURISTIC EVALUATION method (Jakob Nielsen, 10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design, 1994; Nielsen Norman Group):
Walk the interface against ten rules of thumb, derived from factor analysis of 249 real usability problems. Evaluate independently first, then merge — a single reviewer finds a minority of the defects.
1. VISIBILITY OF SYSTEM STATUS
2. MATCH BETWEEN SYSTEM AND THE REAL WORLD
3. USER CONTROL AND FREEDOM (undo, exit, no dead ends)
4. CONSISTENCY AND STANDARDS
5. ERROR PREVENTION (constrain before you warn)
6. RECOGNITION RATHER THAN RECALL
7. FLEXIBILITY AND EFFICIENCY OF USE (accelerators for experts, invisible to novices)
8. AESTHETIC AND MINIMALIST DESIGN
9. HELP USERS RECOGNIZE, DIAGNOSE, AND RECOVER FROM ERRORS (plain language, cause, next action)
10. HELP AND DOCUMENTATION (findable in context, task-focused)
Procedure: pick the two or three real tasks the product exists for. Pass through each screen twice — once for flow, once for detail. Log every issue as: location, the heuristic violated, what the user does wrong because of it, and severity 0–4 (0 not a problem, 1 cosmetic, 2 minor, 3 major, 4 catastrophic — blocks the task). Sort by severity, not by screen.
Deliver a severity-ranked findings list with one concrete fix per finding, plus the two or three violations that repeat across screens — those are system-level, fix them once in the pattern.
