---
when: a design, layout, page, or component set has accumulated elements, decoration, or feature surface and the question is what to remove rather than what to add
---

TEN PRINCIPLES FOR GOOD DESIGN method (Dieter Rams, Braun/Vitsœ; *Weniger, aber besser* — less, but better):
Judge the artifact against ten principles, each as a pass/fail with evidence, not as inspiration.
1. INNOVATIVE — does it use a capability now available, or imitate a form for its own sake?
2. USEFUL — name the use. Everything that does not serve it is decoration.
3. AESTHETIC — is the visual quality intentional, given that only well-executed objects can be beautiful?
4. UNDERSTANDABLE — does the structure explain its own function, ideally making explanation unnecessary?
5. UNOBTRUSIVE — is it neutral and restrained, leaving room for the user's own expression? Products fulfilling a purpose are tools, not decorative objects.
6. HONEST — does it avoid making the product seem more powerful or valuable than it is, and promise nothing it cannot keep?
7. LONG-LASTING — strip anything that is only current fashion; it will date first.
8. THOROUGH TO THE LAST DETAIL — nothing arbitrary: name every value chosen by chance (spacing, weight, radius, easing) and derive it.
9. ENVIRONMENTALLY FRIENDLY — extend to resource cost: payload, energy, and the user's attention.
10. AS LITTLE DESIGN AS POSSIBLE — less, but better; concentrate on the essential.
Procedure: score each principle pass/fail with the specific element that fails it, then produce a REMOVAL LIST — elements deleted, not restyled — and one sentence on what became more legible because of each deletion. A pass with nothing removed is a failed audit.
