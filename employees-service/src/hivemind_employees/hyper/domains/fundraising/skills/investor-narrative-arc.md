when: building a pitch deck, investor memo, or the story a raise will be told with

INVESTOR NARRATIVE ARC (Sequoia Capital, *Writing a Business Plan*; Duarte, *Resonate* — the Sparkline):
Order is the argument. Sequoia's sequence, each section earning the next:
1. COMPANY PURPOSE — one declarative sentence.
2. PROBLEM — the customer's pain, and what they do about it today.
3. SOLUTION — the value proposition, where the product physically sits, real use cases.
4. WHY NOW — the category's evolution and the recent change that makes this possible now. Without a why-now you are pitching a market that already had time to solve this.
5. MARKET SIZE — TAM top-down and SAM bottom-up from real customer counts and price.
6. COMPETITION — named rivals, including the spreadsheet, the intern, and doing nothing.
7. PRODUCT — line-up, architecture, IP, roadmap.
8. BUSINESS MODEL — pricing, average account size, distribution motion.
9. TEAM — why these founders on this problem.
10. FINANCIALS — P&L, cash, cap table, and the deal.
Then impose the Sparkline: alternate WHAT IS (the status quo and its cost, concrete and uncomfortable) against WHAT COULD BE (the world after), and close on the call to adventure — the ask. Contrast carries the persuasion; a monotone feature recital does not.
Discipline: every number sourced or labelled a model. No invented logos, pipeline, partnerships, or "in talks with". If a claim would not survive being read aloud to the customer it describes, cut it.
