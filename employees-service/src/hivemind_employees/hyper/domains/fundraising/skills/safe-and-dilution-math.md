when: pricing a round, issuing a convertible instrument, or stating any post-round ownership percentage
---

SAFE & DILUTION MATH (Y Combinator post-money SAFE, ycombinator.com/documents — the standard since 2018):
1. IDENTIFY THE INSTRUMENT — YC ships cap-only, discount-only and MFN-only post-money SAFEs, plus an optional pro-rata side letter. A SAFE is not debt: no interest, no maturity, no repayment.
2. READ THE CONVERSION TERM — a valuation cap sets the maximum price the investor converts at; a discount sets a percentage off the priced round's price. Where a SAFE carries both, the investor takes whichever yields more shares — never both stacked.
3. USE THE POST-MONEY DEFINITION — post-money SAFE ownership is a fixed percentage of the post-conversion company: investment ÷ post-money cap. Consequence: SAFEs do not dilute each other. Each later SAFE's percentage is absorbed by common — founders and the option pool. Stacked SAFEs at different caps is the standard route to an unexpected Series A ownership number.
4. MODEL BEFORE SIGNING — build the fully diluted post-conversion table for every new instrument: all SAFEs converted, the priced round, and the option-pool refresh. If the Series A pool top-up sits inside the pre-money valuation, common absorbs it while SAFE percentages stay protected.
5. FLAG MFN AND PRO-RATA — an MFN SAFE inherits any better terms granted later, so a low subsequent cap applies retroactively. Pro-rata rights consume allocation in the next round.
6. REPORT AS A RANGE — every ownership figure carries its assumptions (cap, amount, pool size) and names the round it is measured at. Never quote a bare percentage.
