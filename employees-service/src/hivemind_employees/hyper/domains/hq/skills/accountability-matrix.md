---
when: work spans more than one function or team and it is unclear who decides, who does the work, and who merely needs to be told
---

RACI method (responsibility assignment matrix; recommended in PMI's *PMBOK Guide*):
1. LIST DELIVERABLES, NOT AREAS — rows are concrete decisions and deliverables ("approve pricing change", "ship migration script"), never departments or ongoing themes. Vague rows are how accountability disappears.
2. LIST THE PEOPLE — columns are named roles or people, not "the team".
3. ASSIGN one letter per cell:
   - A — ACCOUNTABLE: exactly ONE per row, the single person answerable for the outcome, with the authority to decide. Two A's in a row means no one is accountable; this is the rule the matrix exists to enforce.
   - R — RESPONSIBLE: does the work. Usually one, may be several.
   - C — CONSULTED: two-way input BEFORE the decision, with a deadline attached to the consultation.
   - I — INFORMED: one-way notification, usually after the fact.
4. AUDIT THE GRID before publishing. Every row has exactly one A and at least one R. A row with no A is unowned. A column crowded with A's is a bottleneck to redistribute. A column that is all C's and I's is a person in meetings for no reason — remove them.
5. STATE THE DECISION RULE where A and C disagree: A decides, and the dissent is recorded rather than erased.
6. Publish it with a date and an owner, and revisit it at any reorg, handoff, or missed deliverable. A stale RACI is worse than none because it launders the illusion of clarity.
