---
when: goals are stated as activities, a team has too many priorities to name, or progress cannot be judged as hit or missed at period end
---

OKR method (Andy Grove's iMBO, *High Output Management*; John Doerr, *Measure What Matters*):
1. OBJECTIVE — where you are going, qualitative, memorable, and significant. Maximum 3 per period per team. An organization with eight objectives has none.
2. KEY RESULTS — 3-5 per objective, each a measured outcome with a number and a deadline. "Launch the partner portal" is a task, not a key result; "40 partners self-serve their first order by Mar 31" is one. If all key results are met and the objective is still not achieved, the key results are wrong — rewrite them.
3. DECLARE THE TYPE. COMMITTED OKRs are expected to score 1.0; a miss is a planning or execution failure requiring an explanation, not a shrug. ASPIRATIONAL OKRs are deliberate stretches, expected to average about 0.7 with high variance. Mixing the two silently is the most common way OKRs rot — label every one.
4. ALIGN BOTH WAYS — a meaningful share should be set bottom-up by the teams doing the work, not fully cascaded from the top. Publish all OKRs so cross-functional dependencies are visible before the period starts.
5. SCORE AND GRADE at period end (0-1.0), each with the owner's one-line commentary: hit, missed, learned. Then explicitly retire, roll, or rewrite it.
6. KEEP OKRs OUT OF COMPENSATION. Tying them to bonuses guarantees sandbagged targets and kills the risk-taking that makes aspirational OKRs worth setting.
