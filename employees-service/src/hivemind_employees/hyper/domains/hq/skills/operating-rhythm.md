---
when: the company has priorities and plans but no reliable cadence for driving or correcting them, or issues surface too late to fix inside the quarter
---

MEETING RHYTHM method (Verne Harnish, *Mastering the Rockefeller Habits* / *Scaling Up*):
1. PICK THE QUARTERLY PRIORITY — at most 5 priorities for the quarter, with ONE ranked #1, and one CRITICAL NUMBER that says whether the quarter worked. Deciding the handful of quarterly priorities is the highest-leverage decision leadership makes; more than five means none.
2. NAME AN OWNER AND A METRIC per priority. Unowned or unmeasured priorities are aspirations.
3. RUN THE NESTED CADENCE, on the calendar, without exception — the rhythm working is worth more than any single meeting being good:
   - DAILY HUDDLE, under 15 minutes: what's up, the daily metric, and "where are you stuck". Stucks are surfaced here, never solved here.
   - WEEKLY, 60-90 minutes: one topic of real substance, priority progress, and customer/employee feedback. This is the problem-solving meeting.
   - MONTHLY, a half or full day for managers: resolve the big issues, transfer strategy and judgment down a level.
   - QUARTERLY AND ANNUAL, offsite: reset priorities against the four decisions — Strategy, Execution, People, Cash.
4. ESCALATION PATH — anything unstuck at the daily huddle goes to the weekly with a named owner; anything unresolved weekly goes monthly. Nothing waits for the quarterly by accident.
5. MAINTAIN THE FEEDBACK LOOP — keep qualitative customer and employee data as current as your financials, and report it in the weekly. It is the leading indicator the numbers lag.
6. CLOSE THE QUARTER honestly: did the critical number move, and what does the next quarter's #1 priority become because of it?
