---
when: a process, product, feature, or vendor will handle personal data of people in the EU/EEA
---

GDPR PROCESSING SCREEN — cite the Article, never a vague "GDPR requires":
1. ROLE AND BASIS — controller, joint controller, or processor for this activity, then the Art. 6 lawful basis (plus an Art. 9 condition for special-category data, Art. 8 for children). One basis per purpose. Consent and legitimate interests are not interchangeable, and legitimate interests needs a documented balancing test.
2. RECORD — the activity recorded per Art. 30: purposes, categories of data and subjects, recipients, transfers, retention.
3. PROCESSOR CHAIN — every vendor under an Art. 28(3) DPA: purpose limits, confidentiality, Art. 32 security measures, sub-processor authorisation, assistance duties, deletion or return on termination. Sub-processors listed. Accountability stays with the controller.
4. DPIA TRIGGER — Art. 35(1) where processing is likely high risk, and mandatory under Art. 35(3) for systematic automated decision-making or profiling with legal or similarly significant effect; large-scale special-category or criminal-offence data; large-scale systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area. Also check the relevant supervisory authority's own mandatory-DPIA list.
5. TRANSFERS — for data leaving the EEA: an adequacy decision, or an Art. 46 safeguard, normally the 2021 SCCs with the correct module (C2C / C2P / P2P / P2C). Post-Schrems II (CJEU C-311/18) add a transfer impact assessment following EDPB Recommendations 01/2020 plus supplementary measures; encryption with keys held in the EEA is the most defensible.
6. RIGHTS AND BREACH — a working route for Arts. 15–22 within one month, and a breach path meeting Art. 33 (72 hours to the supervisory authority, with reasons if late) and Art. 34 notice to individuals.
Output: obligation, Article, current state, gap, owner, deadline, residual risk. Mark items needing qualified privacy-counsel sign-off; never certify compliance.
