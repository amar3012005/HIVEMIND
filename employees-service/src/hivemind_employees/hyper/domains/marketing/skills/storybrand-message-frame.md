---
when: copy, a landing page, an email, a script, or an announcement is about to talk about the company instead of the customer
---

STORYBRAND SB7 method (Miller, *Building a StoryBrand*): take the positioning statement as input and convert it into narrative copy. The status inversion is the whole method — the customer is the hero, the brand is only the guide. Any sentence where the company is the hero is a defect.
1. CHARACTER — one customer, one thing they want. Not a persona sketch; a want stated in their words.
2. PROBLEM at three levels — EXTERNAL (the tangible obstacle), INTERNAL (how it makes them feel), PHILOSOPHICAL (why it is wrong that this is hard). Copy that names only the external problem is a spec sheet, and it does not move anyone.
3. GUIDE — empathy plus authority, in that order. Authority is evidence: named customers in their segment, numbers, credentials. Never invent proof, testimonials, logos, or results.
4. PLAN — three or four named steps that remove the risk of starting. Confusion is the real competitor.
5. CALL TO ACTION — exactly one DIRECT CTA (buy, book, request), repeated; plus one TRANSITIONAL CTA (guide, teardown, recorded walkthrough) for those not ready. Two competing direct CTAs is zero CTAs.
6. FAILURE — the concrete cost of doing nothing, tied to a dated trigger. Stakes stated once, not fearmongered.
7. SUCCESS — the specific after-state, not an adjective.
Then compress to a one-liner: "Most [character] struggle with [problem]. We [plan] so they [success]."
GRUNT TEST — a stranger must state what you offer, how it helps, and how to buy it within seconds of the hero section. Fail the test, rewrite the hero section, not the body copy.
