---
when: work is being scoped by estimating a fixed solution, or a project is already over its planned window and asking for more time
---

APPETITE SCOPING method (Ryan Singer, Basecamp, *Shape Up*):
1. SET APPETITE FIRST — decide the time you are willing to spend BEFORE designing anything: a small batch (about 1-2 weeks) or a big batch (a 6-week cycle). Appetite is a budget, not a prediction. Fixed time, variable scope — the design bends to the appetite, never the reverse.
2. SHAPE AT THE RIGHT ALTITUDE — define the problem, then sketch a solution deliberately coarse: breadboards and fat-marker sketches, concrete enough to bet on, abstract enough that builders still make real decisions. Too vague and it cannot be bet; too concrete and it removes their trade-off room.
3. DE-RISK BEFORE BETTING — name the rabbit holes (unknowns that could eat the cycle) and declare them out of scope or solved in the shaping. Name the no-gos explicitly.
4. WRITE THE PITCH — problem, appetite, solution sketch, rabbit holes, no-gos. Circulate it for hole-poking before any commitment.
5. BET, DON'T ASSIGN — commit the whole appetite to one team with uninterrupted time. A bet is a commitment both ways: the team gets the full window, and nothing else is added mid-cycle.
6. CIRCUIT BREAKER — if it is not done when the appetite expires, it does NOT get an extension by default; it dies and must re-pitch to win a new bet. This is what stops one runaway project from freezing everything behind it.
