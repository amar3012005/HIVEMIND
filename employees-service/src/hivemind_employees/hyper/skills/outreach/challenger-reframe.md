when: writing outreach angles, cold email subjects, or a voice-call opening for a strategy's next motions

COMMERCIAL TEACHING method (Dixon & Adamson, *The Challenger Sale*; Rackham, *SPIN Selling*):
Buyers do not reply to introductions, they reply to a reframe — something true about their world they had not priced in.
Sequence: WARMER (show you know their segment) → REFRAME (the uncomfortable insight) → RATIONAL DROWNING (the number that makes it concrete) → EMOTIONAL IMPACT (what it costs THEM personally) → NEW WAY → YOUR SOLUTION. Lead with the reframe, never the product.
Per ICP produce: a subject line naming the trigger, not the product; an opening line that earns the reply by stating the reframe in one sentence; the number that makes it concrete.
For voice calls, use SPIN to structure the goal: SITUATION (confirm the trigger applies), PROBLEM (surface the consequence), IMPLICATION (extend it to cost and blame), NEED-PAYOFF (let them state the value). The call goal is a stated implication, never a demo booking.
Never open with "I hope this finds you well", the company boilerplate, or a feature list. If the opening line would work for any company in any segment, it is wrong.
