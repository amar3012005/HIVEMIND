---
when: define or sharpen positioning, a value proposition, or a category claim
---

POSITIONING method (Dunford, *Obviously Awesome*), in order — never start at the category:
1. COMPETITIVE ALTERNATIVES — what the buyer would truly do instead, including a spreadsheet, an intern, or nothing. Positioning is meaningless without this.
2. UNIQUE ATTRIBUTES — what you have that the alternatives cannot claim. Capabilities, not adjectives.
3. VALUE — the attribute translated into what the buyer gets. Each attribute must map to a value or it is decoration.
4. BEST-FIT CUSTOMERS — who cares about that value URGENTLY. Their urgency is the trigger.
5. MARKET CATEGORY — the frame that makes the value obvious. Pick the frame you can lead in, not the biggest one.
Then write: "For [best-fit], unlike [alternative], we are the [category] that [value] because [attribute]."
Focus law (Ries & Trout): own ONE word in the buyer's head. Two words is zero words. Better to lead a category you named than place fifth in one you did not.
